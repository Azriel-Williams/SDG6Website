Why it Matters

Water and sanitation shortfalls have cascading effects on health, education, and the economy.

  • 0 People die each year from diarrheal diseases linked to unsafe water, sanitation, and hygiene. Preventable with WASH. Improved WASH access reduces disease burden, healthcare costs, and absenteeism, particularly among children.
  • 0 Estimated annual global economic losses from poor sanitation and water‑related health impacts. Losses include lower productivity, missed school days, and higher medical spending, disproportionately affecting the poor.
  • 0 People still lack safely managed drinking water services worldwide. Rural and informal urban settlements face the largest deficits; climate variability increases service instability.

Regional Lens: Caribbean & Small Island States

Unique exposure to hurricanes, drought cycles, and saltwater intrusion raises costs and complexity.

Climate Exposure

Intense storms damage intakes, pumps, and pipe networks; extended dry spells reduce reservoir yields and groundwater recharge.

  • Resilient siting & redundancy
  • Leak reduction to conserve supply
  • Drought‑ready demand management

Small‑System Economics

Smaller populations limit economies of scale for treatment plants; importing parts raises O&M costs and downtime.

  • Regional procurement & spares pools
  • Operator training & remote support
  • Fit‑for‑purpose tech (e.g., UV, RO)
